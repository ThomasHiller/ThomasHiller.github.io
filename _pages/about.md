I am an ecologist with a PhD from Ulm University, completed in collaboration with the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute (STRI) in Panama. I have postdoctoral research experience at the University of Hohenheim and Ulm University. My research examines how land-use change, habitat fragmentation and agricultural management affect biodiversity, species interactions and ecological functions.

Birds and bats are my principal study systems. I investigate how local habitat conditions and the surrounding landscape shape their communities, and how functional traits help explain why species respond differently to environmental change. My work also examines ecological interactions, including plant–frugivore and bat–parasite networks, and their responses to habitat loss and fragmentation.

I combine field surveys, passive acoustic monitoring and camera traps with spatial analyses and ecological modelling. My research spans temperate and tropical ecosystems, with fieldwork in Germany, Panama, Costa Rica and South Africa. An increasing focus of my work is landscape-wide acoustic monitoring, using arrays of recorders to distinguish local habitat effects from those of the wider landscape.

Alongside my research, I teach ecology and biodiversity conservation and supervise student projects. I am interested in connecting ecological research with conservation practice and the management of agricultural and forest landscapes.
